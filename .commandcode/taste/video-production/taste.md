# Taste

## Daily-news video brand
- When commissioning a daily-news short, the user may name only the lead story and delegate the rest: the agent is expected to independently research and pick the other stories (fresh, not repeated from prior editions, credible-sourced) without asking which ones to use. Confidence: 0.7
- The news channel has a fixed visual identity: a green globe on a green background ("green atlas globe" look), used consistently across Shorts and long-form templates. Confidence: 0.95
- Every news video opens with the signature hook — "Good Morning Good News" (positive) or "Good Morning Bad News" (adverse) — as a big, imposing title; the hook appears only at the start and no other headline shares that opening screen. Confidence: 0.95
- In the opening shot the globe is centered in the middle of the frame with the "Good Morning…" title centered inside it ("Good Morning" on one line, "Good News"/"Bad News" on the next). Confidence: 0.9
- Shorts must contain exactly three of the day's most trending stories, and no story should be repeated across runs/videos. Confidence: 0.9
- Long-form videos should go deep on a single story and be dynamic — using globe animations (routes, lines, zooms) in the style of Vox to explain details. Confidence: 0.85
- "Today's 2 shorts" means both daily editions: one constructive ("good") and one adverse ("bad") short, each holding exactly three stories; when one edition is missing, the agent is expected to author and render it in full, not just deliver what already exists. Confidence: 0.85
- A single production run may bundle multiple short formats at once — e.g., "two good news bad news three stories short ... and 4 standalone shorts" was fulfilled as 2 three-story daily editions (one good, one bad) plus 4 single-story standalone shorts, all authored, narrated, and rendered in one pipeline. The terse commissioning language is expected to be interpreted as a combined multi-format batch. Confidence: 0.7
- Standalone-short batches are commissioned with explicit good/bad counts — may be even or uneven (e.g., "make 10 standalone ones, five good five bad" or "make 9 more ... 4 bad 5 good") — and expects the agent to honor the exact requested split; the agent is expected to author, image, narrate, validate, and render the full set end-to-end without confirming the split, pairing each story with a semantically appropriate globe animation. Confidence: 0.75
- Prefers each edition's three stories to span different world regions (e.g., South America + Europe + South Asia) — deliberately swapped a second UK story for an India one to get geographic variety, not just topical variety. Confidence: 0.8
- Has a standing daily production target of 12 shorts per day ("always remember we do 12 a day — or at least try to"): when asked for "six more," the agent is expected to fill today's remaining gap autonomously (e.g., 3 good + 3 bad standalones), keep the batch numbering convention going, and treat the quota as an ongoing standing rule to remember across sessions. Confidence: 0.9

## Globe behavior
- Animation/rendering code must be deterministic and reproducible: no `Math.random`, `Date.now`, `performance.now`, timers, or network URLs in renderers — at most a seeded deterministic PRNG/hash — so renders are bit-reproducible and free of entropy. Confidence: 0.85
- Rotate and zoom the globe to a country as soon as it is mentioned, and highlight every country mentioned in a story, not just the primary one — this applies to the daily globe-tour Shorts too: the user reported missing country highlights as a defect ("why arent the contires being heighlighted?"), so the highlight fill is an expected, must-not-regress visual. Confidence: 0.98
- Zoom into the country being discussed, then zoom back out before rotating to the next country. Confidence: 0.85
- When zooming, the globe must stay anchored in frame (centered or center-left/right as designed) — it should not drift out of the video. Confidence: 0.85
- The globe must be a single continuous instrument across a long-form episode: between chapter animations it should rotate/turn from one location to the next, and must never be swapped for a different globe/map representation that pops in — the user reported a globe-swap between animations ("it switches to a new globe") and a centering drift as a defect and demanded it never happen again. Confidence: 0.85
- Wants the globe-map animation library used whenever possible across all productions — long-form episodes and Shorts alike — ("try to use the animation library whenever possible", "use animations library wherever needed"): expects explicit per-chapter animation plans authored from library presets (shipping-lanes, chokepoint-focus, route-disruption, flow-shift, great-circle-route) rather than static visuals or reliance on the automatic fallback, and favors picking banked topics that showcase the library (e.g., a maritime-routes story over a less geographic one). Confidence: 0.9
- When a standalone feels flat, prefers a punchier re-cut with a tighter geographic zoom and hero subject as visual anchor — explicitly asked to "zoom in and show the ship and stuff" for the Hormuz tanker story, fulfilled as a closer focusZoom (e.g., 6.2 with region-dive → chokepoint-focus) plus a full-bleed Ken Burns push-in on the ship photo so the subject fills the frame rather than a distant globe with static card. Confidence: 0.82
- Requires visual frame verification of every globe render before shipping: expects frames extracted at multiple timestamps across the timeline (e.g., 0.5s, 2s, 5s, 8s, 12s, 16s) plus cropped globe-area inspection, checking that the globe is not clipped at canvas/top-rail edges, stays centered/anchored, annotations and chokepoint markers remain fully visible, and focusZoom keeps the target geography legible — too-tight zooms that push the strait off-center or clip labels are treated as defects to be fixed and re-rendered. Confidence: 0.85

## Images
- Use real photos pulled from the news articles themselves; never generate art-style images — only generate a realistic image as a last resort when no relevant photo exists. Confidence: 0.95
- Show one image at a time as the center focus above the headline; text must not cover the image. Confidence: 0.9
- Don't stretch or letterbox images (no green bars on the sides) — crop/zoom to fill; use multiple topic-specific photos per story. Confidence: 0.9
- Requires fresh, story-specific images fetched from the internet for every new story — never reuse placeholder images from old stories; explicitly instructed to always fetch relevant, story-specific images for each new story rather than reusing prior assets. Confidence: 0.93
- Visually verify every sourced news image before shipping (vision check the actual file): confirm the photo is genuinely on-topic for the story and of adequate resolution — low-res thumbnails (e.g., a 240px frame) and off-point photos (e.g., a crowned crane instead of the secretarybird chick, a maritime-accident scene instead of an Iran missile display) were rejected and replaced with higher-res, on-point alternatives. Confidence: 0.8
- Any generated image for Shorts must be vertical (9:16) format. Confidence: 0.8
- Animate infographics and numbers (bar graphs, stats) and position them above the headline. Confidence: 0.8

## Captions
- Captions should be dynamic/karaoke-style, with words appearing one after another as the center focus of the screen. Confidence: 0.9
- Captions go below the headline, not at the bottom of the video; keep them in a straight line (not tilted), not overlapping images; highlight only key words (e.g., red). Confidence: 0.85
- Keep captions sentence-case and at most two lines. Confidence: 0.6
- For collage/fast-cut videos: expressive dynamic captions where words vary in size/font and some words go behind the images — but keep captions on a straight line, not tilted, and not overlapping images. Confidence: 0.8

## Collage-style videos
- Use relevant full photos from the internet (not just cutouts) that match the script, including identifiable badges/logos when institutions are mentioned. Confidence: 0.75
- Make the images the centerpiece of the frame and keep captions simple. Confidence: 0.75

## Narration & structure
- Use the best available TTS voice; narration for Shorts should stay at or below 18 seconds. Confidence: 0.8
- Short narration scripts have a hard word budget: the story validator enforces a script length cap (scripts exceeding ~44 words failed validation and had to be trimmed to pass), so standalone/short scripts are written deliberately short and within the validator's limit from the start. Confidence: 0.6
- Never read out news sources/resources in the narration — keep credits and source URLs in the description/metadata only. Confidence: 0.9
- End videos with a short voiceover CTA such as "Follow for tomorrow's global brief. Sources in description." and keep footer/credits visible above YouTube's bottom UI. Confidence: 0.75
- Research must use credible sources (ideally at least two per story), verify facts (dates, names, places, numbers), and avoid invented facts and clickbait. Confidence: 0.9

## Long-form explainer workflow
- When commissioning a long-form video, the user may delegate topic selection entirely ("make a long form video on any of the banked topics") and expects the agent to pick a fresh topic and run the whole pipeline — research, story JSON, narration script, TTS, captions, images with provenance, render, visual QA — without further direction. Confidence: 0.8
- When the agent offers topic options with a recommendation, the user makes their own choice and may pick a non-recommended option (e.g., chose the trade-trust topic over the recommended AI-electricity topic, and later chose China's Rare-Earth Leverage over the recommended AI's Electricity Problem even when the pick needed more fresh research) — present genuine options with tradeoffs rather than a foregone conclusion. Confidence: 0.7
- When offered the choice, prefers automated end-to-end execution that delivers one finished video over co-building pass-by-pass or a live storyboard sign-off — selected "Automated end-to-end" and "One finished video" over the alternative run shapes. Confidence: 0.8
- Strongly endorses building videos by strictly following the existing composition template: clone the proven template, retitle/relabel the graphic panels, swap in new data/narration/images, and leave renderer/CSS/globe/timing logic untouched — asked to confirm ("you just followed the template right?") and said "i loved the video." Confidence: 0.9
- Wants the shared video template improved at the root cause before scaling production ("finishing touches to the template before we make more videos") — polish/fix the reusable template once (shared renderer, base CSS) so every current and future episode inherits the fix, rather than patching individual videos. Confidence: 0.75
- Treats text flash-on-entry as a bug to eliminate: whenever any text first appears (opener title, chapter headlines, graphic labels, live captions) it must fade in cleanly with no one-frame flash — the user spots single-frame artifacts and diagnoses them as transition-wiring issues. Confidence: 0.8
- Wants graphs/charts in the long-form template more readable (larger type, thicker chart strokes, higher contrast) — but explicitly rejects pulsing/breathing/looping decorative animations ("no i dont want pulsing graphs"): any yoyo/repeat loops (pulsing endpoint dots, callout breathes, stamp glows, arrow nudges) must be removed, while the readability pass stays. Confidence: 0.9
- Considers the current template layout final ("the positioning of everything is perfect") — polish passes should change typography, contrast, and motion, but must not move or reflow the existing positioning. Confidence: 0.6
- Keep episode-specific composition copy (opener titles, end-card CTA, captions) data-driven in the story JSON with a fallback rather than hardcoded in the shared template/build script — when a build script hardcoded the end-card line, changed it to read `story.ctaLine || default` so episodes don't require per-episode template edits. Confidence: 0.6

## Asset management
- Maintain a shared image library across episodes: before sourcing new images, check existing project folders for reusable photos (e.g., US Capitol, Shanghai skyline reused across multiple explainers) rather than re-fetching identical subjects. Confidence: 0.7
- Every image used in a production must be documented in a per-story provenance manifest (source ledger) recording its origin URL, credit line, and license — explicitly instructed to always record image sources in the source ledger for each new story. Confidence: 0.92

## Editorial data & visual selection
- A globe/map (or any visual treatment) should be used only when it materially improves understanding of the story — automatic selection must be conservative and never decorative. Confidence: 0.9
- Production visuals must use verified data only (coordinates, locations, routes, values, dates, source attribution); never display illustrative/sample data — if required data is missing or invalid, gracefully fall back to another visual treatment instead of fabricating. Confidence: 0.95
- Demo/illustrative content must be clearly labeled as such (e.g., "ILLUSTRATIVE DEMO — NOT REPORTING") so it can never be mistaken for reporting or accidentally shipped to production. Confidence: 0.8
- Frame each aspect ratio with its own safe zones and framing (landscape long-form vs portrait Shorts) rather than mechanically cropping one composition to fit the other. Confidence: 0.75
- Compatibility over renaming: keep external APIs, preset IDs, data contracts, file paths, and explicit story-level controls (preset override, custom verified data, disable/fallback) stable; adapt internally to new requirements rather than forcing story/content files to change, and don't do risky mass renames solely for aesthetics. Confidence: 0.85
- Add structural guardrails (catalog-level assertions/tests) so future additions can't accidentally regress the intended architecture — e.g., a new preset that reintroduces a rejected rendering mode must fail validation. Confidence: 0.8
- Prefers varied, engaging animation beats across a batch rather than repeating the same final preset — explicitly endorsed swapping generic `multi-country-sweep` endings for story-specific visuals like `heatmap-bloom` (Amazon fires basin bloom), `spotlight-dim` (Scotland glen), `dot-density` (MenB rollout wave), and `regional-choropleth` (food-price index) to keep each Short visually distinct while staying production-valid. Confidence: 0.8
- Expects the on-screen `edition` / date to reflect the actual publish date (today), not the research/story-file date — treats stale dates (e.g., rendering `09 Aug 2026` stories on `11 Aug 2026`) as a shipping defect requiring the edition field to be patched and the full batch re-rendered. Confidence: 0.85
- When requesting new stories/renders (e.g., "make 9 more of today news", or "I have already uploaded these make new ones"), expects entirely novel stories researched for today, not reuse of prior editions/batches already uploaded — treats reused past stories as a defect and expects fresh research distinct from story-history.json. Confidence: 0.95
